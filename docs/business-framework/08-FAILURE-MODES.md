# 08 - What Kills One-Person Businesses

## The Five Traps (and How to Avoid Each One)

Every failed solo business dies from one of these five causes. Not market
conditions. Not bad luck. These five things.

---

## Trap 1: Scope Creep

### What It Looks Like

```
Client: "Can you also update the headline on our homepage?"
You: "Sure, no problem."

Client: "While you're at it, can you look at our email subject lines?"
You: "Yeah, I can take a quick look."

Client: "Oh, and my business partner wants to add a few more pages to the audit."
You: "...okay."

RESULT: A $750 project consumed 18 hours instead of 3.
Your effective rate went from $250/hour to $42/hour.
You're exhausted, resentful, and the client thinks this is normal.
```

### How to Prevent It

**1. Define scope in writing before any work starts.**
The SOW is not a formality. It's your boundary document. Be specific:
- "Includes: conversion audit of homepage, 3 product pages, cart, and checkout"
- "Does NOT include: implementation, copy rewrites, or additional pages"

**2. Use the Scope Protection Scripts (from Section 02).**
Memorize them. Practice them. They should feel as natural as saying "hello."

**3. The 24-hour rule.**
Never agree to additional scope in the moment. Always say: "Let me review
that and get back to you with a timeline and cost." Then sleep on it.

**4. Track scope per project.**
Simple Notion column: "Scope additions requested." If it's more than 2 per
client, your SOW needs to be more specific.

## Trap 2: Underpricing

### What It Looks Like

```
You: "I charge $500 for a complete website audit."
Prospect: "Our last agency charged $300."
You: "I can do $350."

OR:

You check competitor pricing. They charge $200. You charge $175 to "be
competitive." You're now working for less than a junior freelancer on
Upwork, except you're delivering senior-level work with AI leverage.

OR:

Client loves your work. Renews 6 months in a row. You never raise the price.
Meanwhile, your skills, AI workflows, and delivery speed have doubled.
You're literally getting paid less per unit of value every month.
```

### How to Prevent It

**1. Price on value, not on cost.**
Your cost is irrelevant to the client. What matters: what is the outcome
worth to them? If your audit helps them make $40K more per year, $750 is
a rounding error.

**2. The 50% rule.**
If more than 50% of prospects say yes without hesitation, you're too cheap.
Raise prices until your close rate is 25-40%.

**3. Never negotiate down. Negotiate scope.**
"I can't lower the price, but I can adjust the scope. Here's what the
$350 version looks like [remove deliverables]. Or we stick with the full
package at $500."

**4. Raise prices every quarter.**
Put it on your calendar. Even 10% per quarter compounds to 46% over a year.
Existing clients keep their rate until renewal.

**5. Cost-plus floor.**
Calculate: (Your target hourly rate x estimated hours) + tool costs +
20% buffer = minimum price. Never go below this number.

## Trap 3: Custom Everything

### What It Looks Like

```
Client A wants a LinkedIn content strategy.
Client B wants a Twitter content strategy.
Client C wants a TikTok content strategy.
Client D wants a podcast content strategy.

You build 4 different delivery systems.
You write 4 different sets of prompts.
You create 4 different SOPs.
You learn 4 different platforms.

Your "business" is actually 4 different businesses.
None of them are efficient. All of them are stressful.
```

### How to Prevent It

**1. Productize ruthlessly.**
Pick ONE deliverable format. ONE platform. ONE client type. Repeat it
until you can deliver it in your sleep.

**2. The Menu Rule.**
Would McDonald's let you order a pizza? No. They have a menu. You need a menu.
Tier 1, Tier 2, Tier 3. That's it. If it's not on the menu, it's a "no."

**3. The "I don't do that" muscle.**
Practice saying: "That's outside my focus area. I specialize in [X] for [Y].
I can refer you to someone who handles [what they want]."

You lose one prospect. You protect 100 hours of efficiency.

**4. Custom is a premium.**
If someone truly needs custom work, it's Tier 3 pricing. Custom = premium.
Never custom at Tier 1 prices.

## Trap 4: Bad Clients

### Red Flags (Detect Early, Save Yourself Later)

```
RED FLAG: "Can you do a free trial/test project?"
REALITY: They don't value your work. They'll nickel-and-dime everything.

RED FLAG: "Our last person didn't work out" (said about 3+ past vendors)
REALITY: They're the problem, not the vendors.

RED FLAG: "We need it yesterday."
REALITY: Poor planning on their part will become your emergency every time.

RED FLAG: "We don't have a big budget, but there's tons of future work."
REALITY: There's no future work. There's just today's project at a discount.

RED FLAG: Slow to respond during sales, but expects instant responses after.
REALITY: Asymmetric expectations that will only get worse.

RED FLAG: Wants to negotiate before understanding the offer.
REALITY: They're optimizing for cheapest price, not best outcome.

RED FLAG: Can't clearly articulate what they want.
REALITY: You'll spend more time managing confusion than delivering value.

RED FLAG: CC's 5 people on every email.
REALITY: Decision by committee. Nothing will ever be approved quickly.
```

### The Qualification Process

Before every discovery call, confirm:

```
[ ] They have the budget (ask directly: "This service typically runs $X-Y.
    Does that fit within your budget?")
[ ] They have the authority (ask: "Are you the decision maker, or does
    someone else need to approve?")
[ ] They have the need (ask: "What happens if you don't solve this?")
[ ] They have the timeline (ask: "When do you need this by?")
```

If they fail any of these: thank them, decline politely, move on.

### The Firing Script

When you need to let a bad client go:

```
"[Client name], I've been reviewing my client roster and capacity, and
I've decided to narrow my focus to [specific type of work]. Because of this,
I won't be able to continue our engagement after [date].

I want to make sure you have a smooth transition. I can [recommend someone /
deliver final work by X date / provide all files and documentation].

It's been great working with you, and I wish you the best."
```

No reasons. No arguments. Professional, clean, final.

## Trap 5: Overcomplicating

### What It Looks Like

```
- Building a custom CRM when a spreadsheet works
- Spending 3 weeks on a website when a Carrd page converts fine
- Creating a 12-email nurture sequence before you have 50 subscribers
- Setting up Zapier automations for a process you do twice a month
- Buying 8 AI tools when you use 2 features across all of them
- Designing a logo before you have a single client
- Writing a business plan nobody will read
- Incorporating an LLC before making a dollar
- Building a course platform before validating the topic
- Perfecting your "brand" when nobody knows you exist
```

### How to Prevent It

**1. The Revenue Test.**
Before spending time on anything, ask: "Will this directly generate revenue
in the next 30 days?" If no, it's not a priority. Do the revenue thing first.

**2. The Minimum Viable Everything.**
- MVP website: single page with your offer, testimonials, and a way to contact you
- MVP CRM: Notion table or spreadsheet
- MVP contract: Google Doc with e-signature
- MVP content: text posts (no graphics required)
- MVP portfolio: Notion page with 3 examples

You can upgrade everything later. With revenue.

**3. The 2-Tool Rule.**
For any function, you need a maximum of 2 tools. Writing: Claude + Google Docs.
Not Claude + ChatGPT + Jasper + Copy.ai + Grammarly.

**4. The Saturday Morning Test.**
If you can't explain your business model and workflow to a smart friend
in 5 minutes on a Saturday morning, it's too complicated. Simplify until
you can.

## The Meta-Trap: Confusing Activity with Progress

All five traps above share a common root: they feel productive.

```
Scope creep feels like "going the extra mile."
Underpricing feels like "being competitive."
Custom work feels like "being flexible."
Bad clients feel like "building your portfolio."
Overcomplicating feels like "being thorough."
```

None of these are progress. Progress is measured in:

1. **Revenue** -- did money enter your bank account?
2. **Pipeline** -- are qualified prospects in conversation with you?
3. **Systems** -- can you deliver faster and better than last month?

If your activity isn't moving one of these three numbers, stop doing it
and do something that does.
