## Business Strategy Prompts

### Niche Validator

```
I'm considering starting a business in this niche:

NICHE: [your niche]
OFFER: [what you'd sell]
TARGET CUSTOMER: [who would buy]
PRICE POINT: [$X]

Pressure-test this idea ruthlessly. Analyze:

1. DEMAND: Is there evidence people actively search for and pay for this?
   Provide specific data points (search volume, competitor revenue, market size).

2. COMPETITION: Who are the top 5 players? What do they charge? Where are the
   gaps in their offerings that I could exploit?

3. AI LEVERAGE: What percentage of the delivery can AI handle? Be specific
   about which tasks and which tools.

4. UNIT ECONOMICS: At my price point, how many clients/sales per month to hit
   $10K/month? Is that volume realistic for a solo operator?

5. RISKS: What are the 3 most likely ways this fails? Be honest -- I need
   reasons to NOT do this, not encouragement.

6. VERDICT: On a scale of 1-10, rate this idea's viability for a one-person
   AI-powered business. Explain your rating.

Be direct. No hedging. If this is a bad idea, say so and suggest what would
be a better idea in the same general space.
```

### Competitor Analyzer

```
Analyze these competitors in my market:

MY BUSINESS: [what you do]
MY TARGET CUSTOMER: [your avatar]

COMPETITORS:
1. [Name + URL]
2. [Name + URL]
3. [Name + URL]
4. [Name + URL]
5. [Name + URL]

For each competitor, analyze:
- What they offer (services/products, pricing if visible)
- Their positioning (how they describe themselves)
- Their content strategy (what platforms, what topics, frequency)
- Their apparent target customer
- Their strengths
- Their weaknesses or gaps

Then tell me:
1. Where is the positioning gap? What angle are NONE of them taking?
2. What do their customers likely complain about? (Infer from their reviews,
   testimonials, and offering structure)
3. How should I position against them? Give me a specific positioning
   statement that differentiates me.
4. What's the one thing I could do that they can't or won't?

Output as a comparison table plus strategic recommendations.
```

### Pricing Calculator

```
SERVICE: [what you deliver]
TARGET CUSTOMER: [who buys it]
DELIVERY TIME: [how long it takes you]

3. VALUE-BASED PRICE: Based on client ROI, what's the maximum rational price?
4. RECOMMENDED PRICE: Your recommendation with reasoning

Then design my 3-tier pricing:
- Tier 1 (entry): What to include and price
- Tier 2 (core): What to include and price
- Tier 3 (premium): What to include and price

Show me the revenue math: how many of each tier per month to hit $10K/month.
What's the most realistic mix?
```

## Client Acquisition Prompts

### Outreach Message Generator

```
Write 5 personalized cold outreach messages for my business.

MY SERVICE: [what you offer]
MY TARGET: [specific avatar]
PLATFORM: [LinkedIn DM / email / Twitter DM]
MY DIFFERENTIATOR: [what makes me different]
PROOF POINTS: [any results, testimonials, or credentials]

For each message:
- Open with a personalized observation (I'll fill in the specific detail)
- Name their likely pain point
- Briefly describe my solution (1 sentence)
- End with a low-commitment CTA (not "hop on a call")
- Keep under 100 words
- Tone: professional but human, not salesy

Also write:
- 1 follow-up message (for no response after 3 days)
- 1 follow-up message (for no response after 7 days)
- 1 message for warm leads (they engaged with my content)

Format each message so I can copy-paste with minimal editing.
```

### Content Ideas Generator (30 Days)

```
Generate 30 days of content ideas for my business.

MY BUSINESS: [what I do]
MY AUDIENCE: [who follows me / who I want to attract]
PLATFORM: [LinkedIn / Twitter / both]
MY EXPERTISE: [what I know deeply]
CONTENT GOAL: [generate leads / build authority / drive to lead magnet]

For each day, provide:
- Content type: (insight / how-to / case study / myth-bust / opinion /
  behind-the-scenes / direct CTA)
- Topic: [specific topic, not vague]
- Hook: [first line that stops the scroll -- under 15 words]
- Key points: [3-4 bullet points of what to cover]
- CTA: [what should the reader do after reading]

Distribution:
- 40% problem-aware content (name their pains)
- 30% social proof / results content
- 20% educational / how-to content
- 10% direct offers

Include a mix of short posts (under 200 words) and longer posts (500+ words).
Make the hooks compelling -- no generic "Here's what I learned about X."
```

### Lead Magnet Creator

```
Create a lead magnet for my business that I can build in under 60 minutes.

MY BUSINESS: [what I do]
MY TARGET AUDIENCE: [who downloads this]
THEIR BIGGEST PROBLEM: [the pain they want solved]
FORMAT: [checklist / template / guide / calculator / swipe file]

Create the complete lead magnet content:

1. TITLE: Use the formula "The [Number]-[Type] [Outcome] [Format]"
   Example: "The 15-Point Website Conversion Checklist"

2. SUBTITLE: One sentence describing the specific benefit

3. INTRODUCTION: 2-3 paragraphs explaining why this matters (150 words max)

4. CORE CONTENT: The actual checklist/template/guide content
   - Make each item specific and actionable
   - Include brief explanations for each point (2-3 sentences)
   - Organize logically (by priority, category, or sequence)

5. BONUS SECTION: One additional high-value tip or resource

6. CTA: What should they do next? (book a call, reply to email, check out
   your service)

7. LANDING PAGE COPY: Write the headline, subheadline, 3 bullet points,
   and CTA button text for the download page.

Make it genuinely valuable. If someone gets this for free and implements it,
they should get real results. That's what makes them want to hire you.
```

### Email Sequence Writer (7 Emails)

```
Write a 7-email nurture sequence for my business.

MY BUSINESS: [what I do]
TARGET AUDIENCE: [who signed up]
LEAD MAGNET THEY DOWNLOADED: [what brought them to my list]
MY CORE OFFER: [what I want to sell them]
OFFER PRICE: [$X]
MAIN OBJECTIONS: [why they might not buy]

Write all 7 emails:

EMAIL 1 (Day 0 -- Immediate):
- Subject line: Deliver the lead magnet
- Body: Thank them, deliver the asset, introduce yourself briefly (3 sentences)
- PS: Tease what's coming in the next email

EMAIL 2 (Day 2):
- Subject line: Your story / why you do this
- Body: Brief credibility-building story. How you discovered this problem and
  built your solution. End with a lesson they can apply today.

EMAIL 3 (Day 4):
- Subject line: Teach something valuable
- Body: One actionable insight related to their problem. Something they can
  implement in 15 minutes. Demonstrate your expertise.

EMAIL 4 (Day 7):
- Subject line: Case study / social proof
- Body: Share a specific client result. Problem → solution → outcome format.
  Include specific numbers if possible.

EMAIL 5 (Day 10):
- Subject line: Address main objection
- Body: Name their biggest reason for not taking action. Dismantle it with
  logic, social proof, or reframing.

EMAIL 6 (Day 14):
- Subject line: Soft pitch
- Body: Acknowledge their problem still exists. Present your offer as the
  logical next step. No pressure -- just clarity.

EMAIL 7 (Day 17):
- Subject line: Direct offer + urgency
- Body: Clear pitch. What they get, what it costs, what happens next.
  Include a deadline or scarcity element if genuine.

Requirements for all emails:
- Subject lines under 50 characters
- Preview text included
- Body under 300 words
- Conversational tone (not corporate)
- One clear CTA per email
- Mobile-friendly (short paragraphs, no walls of text)
```

## Service Delivery Prompts

### Client Research Brief

```
Create a comprehensive research brief for my client project.

CLIENT: [company name]
INDUSTRY: [their industry]
WEBSITE: [URL]
PROJECT SCOPE: [what we're delivering]
TARGET AUDIENCE: [their customers]

Research and compile:

1. COMPANY OVERVIEW:
   - What they do (in plain language)
   - Their apparent target market
   - Their positioning/messaging
   - Revenue stage estimate (startup / growth / established)

2. COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE:
   - Top 5 competitors
   - How each competitor positions themselves
   - Where the client has advantages/disadvantages

3. INDUSTRY TRENDS:
   - 3-5 trends affecting their industry right now
   - Opportunities these trends create
   - Threats to be aware of

4. AUDIENCE INSIGHTS:
   - Key pain points of their target audience
   - Common objections when buying their type of product/service
   - Language and terminology their audience uses

5. RECOMMENDATIONS:
   - 3 strategic recommendations based on this research
   - Quick wins they can implement immediately
   - Long-term opportunities to consider

Format as a professional brief I can share directly with the client.
Include citations and sources where possible.
```

### Deliverable Quality Review

```
Review this deliverable for quality before I send it to my client.

CLIENT: [name]
DELIVERABLE TYPE: [what it is]
CLIENT'S INDUSTRY: [industry]
TARGET AUDIENCE: [who this is for]

DELIVERABLE CONTENT:
[paste the full deliverable]

Review against these criteria:

1. ACCURACY: Flag any claims that seem unverified, statistics without sources,
   or potentially incorrect information.

2. RELEVANCE: Is every section relevant to the client's specific situation?
   Flag anything generic that should be customized.

3. ACTIONABILITY: Can the client actually implement these recommendations?
   Flag anything vague that needs specific steps.

4. PROFESSIONALISM: Check for typos, inconsistent formatting, awkward phrasing,
   or tone issues.

5. COMPLETENESS: Is anything missing that the client would expect based on the
   scope of [project type]?

6. SENIOR CRITIQUE: What would a senior [relevant role] criticize about this?
   What would they want to see that's missing?

Output:
- Overall quality score (1-10)
- List of specific issues to fix (categorized as critical / important / minor)
- Suggested improvements for each issue
- Any sections that should be rewritten entirely
```

## Operations Prompts

### SOP Creator

```
Create a Standard Operating Procedure for this process:

PROCESS NAME: [what the SOP covers]
PROCESS GOAL: [what successful completion looks like]
FREQUENCY: [how often this process runs]
TOOLS USED: [list tools involved]

I'll describe the process and you'll turn it into a structured SOP:

[Describe the process in whatever detail you have -- even rough notes work]

Create the SOP with:

1. OVERVIEW: What this process achieves and when to use it
2. PREREQUISITES: What needs to be in place before starting
3. STEPS: Numbered steps with:
   - Action (what to do)
   - Tool (what tool to use)
   - Details (specific instructions, including prompts if AI is involved)
   - Time estimate (minutes)
   - Output (what this step produces)
4. QUALITY CHECK: How to verify the process was done correctly
5. COMMON ISSUES: What might go wrong and how to fix it
6. TOTAL TIME: Estimated time for the full process

Format for easy reference -- someone should be able to follow this without
asking questions. Include screenshots or examples where helpful.
```

## Prompt Engineering Tips

### For Better AI Output

1. **Be specific about format.** "Output as a table" or "Use bullet points"
   gets better results than hoping it picks the right format.

2. **Give examples.** "Like this: [example]" dramatically improves output quality.

3. **Set constraints.** "Under 200 words" or "Exactly 5 items" prevents rambling.

4. **Assign a role.** "You are a senior conversion rate optimizer with 15 years
   of experience" changes the output quality.

5. **Ask for honesty.** "Be brutally honest" and "Tell me why this is a bad idea"
   counteract AI's tendency to agree with everything.

6. **Iterate, don't regenerate.** "Make point 3 more specific" is better than
   rerunning the entire prompt.

7. **Chain prompts.** Complex deliverables should use 3-4 prompts in sequence,
   each building on the last, rather than one mega-prompt.

8. **Save your best prompts.** When a prompt produces exceptional output, save it
   exactly as-is. Don't "improve" prompts that work.
