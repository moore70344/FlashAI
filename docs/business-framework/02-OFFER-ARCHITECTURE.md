# 02 - Offer Architecture

## The Offer That Sells

An offer isn't what you do. It's what the client gets. Nobody buys "consulting."
They buy "a 40% increase in qualified leads within 60 days."

---

## Offer Design Template

### The Core Offer

```
WHAT I'M SELLING:
[Specific deliverable -- not "marketing services" but "a complete LinkedIn
content system that generates 10+ inbound leads per month"]

WHO'S BUYING:
[Exact avatar -- not "small businesses" but "B2B SaaS founders doing $500K-$2M
ARR who post on LinkedIn inconsistently and get zero leads from it"]

PRICE POINT: $[X]
Justification: [This is [X]% of the value delivered. If I generate $Y in
revenue for them, charging $Z is a no-brainer ROI]

DELIVERY TIMELINE: [X days/weeks]

WHAT'S INCLUDED:
- [ ] [Deliverable 1 with specifics]
- [ ] [Deliverable 2 with specifics]
- [ ] [Deliverable 3 with specifics]
- [ ] [Number] of revisions included
- [ ] [Support/communication terms]

WHAT'S NOT INCLUDED (protects your margin):
- [ ] [Boundary 1 -- e.g., "implementation/posting -- we deliver the content"]
- [ ] [Boundary 2 -- e.g., "ad spend or paid promotion"]
- [ ] [Boundary 3 -- e.g., "strategy calls beyond the initial kickoff"]
- [ ] [Boundary 4 -- e.g., "revisions beyond [X] rounds"]
```

## The Offer Stack

The stack makes your offer feel 3-5x more valuable than the price. The key:
bonuses cost you nothing because AI generates them.

```
CORE DELIVERABLE: [What they pay for]
Value: $[perceived value]

BONUS 1: [AI-generated add-on]
- What it is: [e.g., "Custom competitor analysis report"]
- How AI creates it: [e.g., "Claude analyzes top 5 competitors in 10 minutes"]
- Your time: [e.g., "15 minutes to review and customize"]
- Perceived value: $[X]

BONUS 2: [AI-generated add-on]
- What it is: [e.g., "90-day content calendar with post ideas"]
- How AI creates it: [e.g., "Prompt generates 90 topic ideas from client niche"]
- Your time: [e.g., "10 minutes to curate top 30"]
- Perceived value: $[X]

BONUS 3: [AI-generated add-on]
- What it is: [e.g., "Email swipe file with 10 ready-to-send templates"]
- How AI creates it: [e.g., "AI writes templates based on client's offer"]
- Your time: [e.g., "20 minutes to customize"]
- Perceived value: $[X]

GUARANTEE: [Specific, risk-reversing]
- Example: "If you don't get at least [X result] within [Y timeframe],
  I'll [refund / redo the work / continue free until you do]"
- Why this works: [shifts risk from buyer to seller -- you can afford this
  because AI makes redo costs near-zero]

TOTAL PERCEIVED VALUE: $[sum of all above]
PRICE: $[your price]
VALUE-TO-PRICE RATIO: [X]:1
```

## Offer Validation Checklist

Before you sell anything, it must pass all five tests:

- [ ] **Pain test:** Does my target customer actively search for solutions to this
  problem? (Not "would they want this" -- do they already spend money on it?)
- [ ] **Wallet test:** Can my target customer afford my price without committee
  approval? (B2B: under their discretionary spend limit. B2C: under $500.)
- [ ] **Clarity test:** Can I explain what they get in one sentence?
- [ ] **AI test:** Can AI handle 60%+ of the delivery work?
- [ ] **Margin test:** After AI tool costs, is my profit margin above 70%?

If any answer is "no," redesign the offer.

### Service Arbitrage: AI Content Agency

```

PRICE: $2,500/month retainer
DELIVERY: Ongoing monthly

- Paid ads or promotion
- Graphic design beyond text posts

PROFIT MARGIN: 87%
TIME PER CLIENT: 4-5 hours/month
```

## Offer Naming Formula

Your offer name should follow this structure:

**The [Outcome] [Mechanism]**

Examples:
- "The Lead Engine" (not "Social Media Management")
- "The Conversion Audit" (not "Website Review")
- "The Revenue Blueprint" (not "Business Consulting")
- "The Content Machine" (not "Writing Services")

The name implies the result, not the process. Clients buy outcomes.

## Scope Protection Scripts

Use these when clients try to expand scope:

**"Can you also do X?"**
> "That's a great idea and I'd love to help with that. It falls outside the
> current scope, but I can put together a separate proposal for X. Want me
> to send that over?"

**"I thought this was included."**
> "I understand the confusion. Let me pull up the scope we agreed on --
> [reference SOW]. X isn't in the current package, but I can add it as an
> upgrade for $Y. Or we can include it in next month's engagement."

**"My last person did this for free."**
> "I respect that. My process is designed to deliver [specific outcome] and
> the scope is structured to make that happen consistently. If you'd like
> to add X, here's what that looks like as an add-on."

Never say yes to scope expansion in the moment. Always create a separate
line item. This is how you protect margin.
